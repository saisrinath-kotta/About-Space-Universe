Why Do Weights Differ on Different Planets?
🔬 The Gravity Formula: g = GM/r²
Your weight is the gravitational force pulling you toward a planet's core. It depends on the planet's mass (M) and radius (r). G is the universal gravitational constant — the same everywhere in the universe.
🪐 Bigger planet ≠ always heavier you
Saturn is enormous but less dense than Earth — so surface gravity is only slightly above Earth's. Tiny but dense Mars has just 38% of Earth's gravity. The mass-to-radius ratio is everything!
☀️ The Sun's extreme gravitational pull
The Sun contains 99.86% of all solar system mass — you'd weigh ~28× more there. Jupiter, the largest planet, gives you about 2.5× your Earth weight.
⚖️ Mass vs Weight — the key difference
Your mass (amount of matter in your body) never changes — on Earth, Mars, or floating in deep space. Only your weight changes because gravity changes. That's why astronauts are weightless in orbit!

What Happens When You Jump on Different Planets?
📐 The Physics Formula
Jump height scales inversely with surface gravity: h_planet = h_earth × (g_earth ÷ g_planet). Lower gravity means a higher jump!
🌕 On the Moon (gravity = 0.17g)
You'd soar nearly 6× higher! A 50 cm Earth jump becomes ~295 cm. Apollo astronauts could jump over 1 metre even in their heavy 127 kg suits.
🔴 On Mars (gravity = 0.38g)
About 2.6× higher than Earth. Future colonists could bound across the red planet in giant, graceful strides — perfect for low-gravity athletics!
⚠️ On Jupiter (gravity = 2.53g)
You'd barely leave the ground — and the "surface" is crushing gas anyway. The gravity would feel like carrying 2.5× your own body weight at all times.
🌑 On Pluto (gravity = 0.06g)
A 50 cm jump becomes over 8 metres! You'd need to be careful — a running jump could launch you into a very long, slow arc through the thin atmosphere.
