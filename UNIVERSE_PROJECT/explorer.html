Chapter 02

Space Missions

USSR
1957
Sputnik 1
The world's first artificial satellite, launched by the Soviet Union. Its beeping radio signal heard around the world ignited the Space Race and marked the dawn of the space age.
USA
1969
Apollo 11 — Moon Landing
NASA's most iconic mission. Astronauts Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin became the first humans to walk on the Moon while Michael Collins orbited above. A defining moment for all of humanity.
NASA
1977
Voyager 1 & 2
Twin spacecraft launched to explore the outer planets. Voyager 1 has since become the farthest human-made object in history, now traveling through interstellar space over 23 billion km away.
NASA · ESA
1990
Hubble Space Telescope
Launched into low Earth orbit, Hubble revolutionized astronomy with breathtaking images of distant galaxies, nebulae, and deep space — forever changing our understanding of the cosmos.
NASA
2004
Mars Exploration Rovers
Spirit and Opportunity landed on Mars to study its geology and search for signs of past water. Opportunity operated for nearly 15 years — far beyond its 90-day mission plan.
ISRO
2023
Chandrayaan-3
India became the fourth nation to land on the Moon and the first to land near the lunar south pole. The Pragyan rover confirmed the presence of sulfur and other elements — a historic triumph for Indian science.

Chapter 03

Space Stations

International Space Station
A marvel of international cooperation, the ISS has been continuously inhabited since November 2000. Spanning 109 meters — roughly the size of a football field — it orbits Earth at 28,000 km/h and hosts astronauts from 19 countries conducting cutting-edge microgravity research.
Orbital Labs
The ISS hosts over 3,000 research experiments across biology, physics, astronomy, and medicine. Findings have led to breakthroughs in cancer research, water purification, and materials science.
3,000+
research experiments conducted
Mir Station
Soviet/Russian station that operated 1986–2001. The first permanently crewed space station, it hosted 28 long-duration crews and proved humans can live in space long-term.
Tiangong
China's space station, completed in 2022. A T-shaped modular station in low Earth orbit that represents China's independent space ambitions and houses its growing taikonaut program.

Chapter 04

Telescopes & Observatories

Hubble Space Telescope
LAUNCHED 1990 · NASA / ESA
Orbiting 547 km above Earth, Hubble has captured over 1.5 million observations including the famous "Pillars of Creation" and helped determine the age of the universe at 13.8 billion years.
James Webb Space Telescope
LAUNCHED 2021 · NASA / ESA / CSA
The most powerful telescope ever built. Positioned 1.5 million km away, JWST peers into the universe's earliest moments, observing galaxies formed just 300 million years after the Big Bang.
Arecibo Observatory
1963 – 2020 · PUERTO RICO
Once the world's largest single-dish radio telescope at 305 m wide. Used to study pulsars, search for extraterrestrial intelligence (SETI), and even send humanity's first deliberate radio message to space.
Chandra X-ray Observatory
LAUNCHED 1999 · NASA
Designed to detect X-ray emissions from extreme cosmic environments. Chandra has revealed the behavior of matter near black holes, neutron stars, and the superheated gas of galaxy clusters.
Event Horizon Telescope
FIRST IMAGE 2019 · GLOBAL NETWORK
A planet-scale array of radio telescopes that produced the first-ever image of a black hole shadow in galaxy M87, and later of Sagittarius A* — the black hole at the center of our own Milky Way.
Very Large Array (VLA)
SINCE 1980 · NEW MEXICO, USA
27 radio antennas spread across 36 km of New Mexico desert. The VLA has studied everything from black hole jets to the cosmic microwave background — and even appeared in the film Contact.
